# Business metric semantics and versioning

A semantic layer maps a user term to an approved measure, grain, filters, unit and calendar. [The current catalog](../../apps/api/src/insightrag/analytics/planning.py) is deliberately small: `revenue=SUM(signed revenue)`, `profit=SUM(revenue)-SUM(cost)`, `margin=100*profit/SUM(revenue)`, `units=SUM(units)`, `returns=SUM(returns)` and regional `contribution=100*regional revenue/national revenue`. [The schema API](../../apps/api/src/insightrag/analytics/routes.py) exposes descriptions only after query authorization and entitled-row presence. Currency must be one explicit code; no conversion rate is invented. Calendar periods are local to `orgs.reporting_timezone`, with exclusive end dates.

**Why this matters.** “Sales” might mean gross bookings, net revenue, shipped units or cash received. The current contract maps “sales” to signed revenue; a real customer must approve that meaning. `SUM(row margin)/COUNT` is wrong for total margin: two rows of revenue 100/profit 10 and revenue 900/profit 180 have margins 10% and 20%; their unweighted average is 15%, while total profit 190 / total revenue 1000 is **19%**. Revenue from two currencies cannot be summed without an approved conversion table, rate type and effective date.

**Implementation and alternatives.** Fixed Python metric expressions and database decimal types give a reviewable first glossary. Responses carry `metric_definition_version=sales-assumption-v1` and a source-file SHA-256. This is a code-level identifier for *assumed* formulas, not an approved or persisted tenant-specific metric registry. A versioned metadata catalog or dbt/MetricFlow-like semantic layer can support reuse and governed joins later. Audit-grade historical replay still needs immutable stored definitions and authorized contributor lineage; row-number minimum/maximum bounds do not enumerate all contributing rows. Add data dictionaries, approval workflows, FX contracts and period-completeness status before multi-source enterprise BI. Interview defense: a semantic layer is a contract, not a natural-language synonym list.
